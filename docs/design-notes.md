# Design and preview notes · interface v7

The updated theme combines warm cream, coral, soft green, sky blue, and yellow with pixel game artwork, framed game-like panels, and a persistent navigation rail. Discover uses illustrated Pokémon artwork to introduce the launchpad while keeping coin discovery and the main token prominent. Gym rankings live on the Gyms page. Search, notifications, and account access remain in the header. The primary rail order is Discover, Create Coin, Portfolio, Rewards, and Analytics; trainer and league destinations follow.

The custom Poképad mark is separate from the wordmark. The name uses existing Pokémon Solid and Hollow font files rather than newly drawn lettering. Pixel display fonts support labels while ordinary text stays readable. Solana values use the bundled Solana brand mark. Asset origins are recorded in public/assets/ASSET-SOURCES.md.

Discover cards separate the token's uploaded image from its Pokémon mascot. The image or neutral monogram sits with the name and ticker; the larger mascot animates on hover or keyboard focus without changing its frame geometry. Sprite-sheet playback pauses and resumes in place; full-sprite GIF exceptions crossfade between their mounted layers. A compact line graph sits below the mascot. Cards emphasize market cap, with volume, holders, reward mode, social links, age, and an independent watchlist control. Sorting uses a restrained selected treatment. Watchlists are local, wallet-scoped preferences and can filter the discovery list. The sample USD conversion is fixed fixture data, never a live quote. Card trends consume optional ordered `Coin.priceHistory` samples. Known demo coins can display deterministic illustrative trends, labelled in the graphic’s accessible text; missing live history stays empty rather than becoming a fabricated chart.

Pixel Pokémon are the default. All 151 Gen 1 species have local animated sprite sheets derived from their Black/White sprites; these are not original Red/Blue animation frames. Drawn artwork remains available through Settings. Idle animation respects reduced-motion preferences and pauses for offscreen sprites where supported.

The sidebar partner always uses pixel art. It responds to petting, selected reward/evolution/gym events, and periodically offers short tips. Trainer avatars crop classic FireRed/LeafGreen character sprites to shoulder portraits with selectable accessory-colour variants. The character and colour are randomized on the first preview load and persist afterward. Display names fall back to a short wallet address. The header portrait now shows only the trainer. Brock and Blue use pixel-aligned SVG clothing overlays over unchanged source PNGs, with a base colour and darker garment shading so their choices visibly differ without recolouring skin or hair. Pokémon fit their stages using opaque-art bounds rather than the transparent source canvas, and My PC centers the complete silhouette. Normal and shiny palettes remain visible collection variants; Electric badges use a clear yellow.

The startup screen uses the moving Poké Ball alone, without orbiting dots. The wider red Pokédex-inspired header search control uses a blue magnifying-glass lens and opens a smooth dialog with sequential orange/yellow/green lights, keyboard navigation, Escape dismissal, and results for both coins and trainers. Trainer-wallet results open public collection profiles. The demo only knows its fixture trainers and the current account; unknown wallets show a missing-profile state. Connected searches use the public trainer-directory adapter and show errors instead of falling back to fixtures.

Eggs use local pixel art with distinct tier colours, a source-coin badge, a timer, and a walking equivalent. Pack and egg reveals lead into animated Pokémon results with looping stars. Evolution has a separate transformation sequence. Reduced-motion settings shorten or remove motion. Source text is kept to “From” and the coin identity.

The How-to guide replaces the short three-page manual with nine searchable categories: getting started; coins and launching; eggs, packs, and walking; collection; training; value and marketplace; gyms; battles; and account preferences. Each category uses a relevant static Pokémon illustration, readable explanations, and direct links to the screens it describes. The full guide is also embedded in account help. It distinguishes intended live mechanics from actions supported by this frontend. Generic all-caps promotional subtitles and decorative sparkle glyphs are absent from shared page headings.

Analytics combines compact snapshot metrics, a coin-volume ranking, a reward-mode breakdown, and a filterable event list. The account activity tab reflects recorded actions. A separate Community preview tab labels its example pack reveals, hatches, purchases, evolutions, and launches; these examples do not affect the totals. Snapshot coverage and free-pool accounting are explained next to the figures.

Inventory means currently owned Pokémon; the Pokédex remembers every discovered species. Pokémon detail keeps a full sprite, identity, HP, value, and primary actions beside a Stats/Moves/Evolution/Details button menu. Information appears one section at a time, reducing vertical travel. The styled move-ready prompt identifies the offered move and its type and opens the existing learning dialog. It retains an explicit back button, Rare Candy progress, funded evolution readiness, and one-time move-learning prompts. A player can learn into an empty slot, replace one of four moves, or permanently decline. Eevee's outcome is random rather than a selectable branch. See the backend contract for funding and progression rules; these are not the original games' complete mechanics.

Create coin is a dedicated navigation destination with four steps: Details, Partner, Rewards, and Review. Details groups Coin and Links subpanels, so social fields no longer have a separate step. Partner always offers the current trainer as well as eligible owned Pokémon. Back and Next occupy a persistent footer outside the step’s scroll region; compact layouts and contained reward illustrations keep controls reachable without page scrolling on typical desktop/laptop sizes. Smaller viewports retain internal scrolling rather than clipping fields. Its previews animate; static shiny assets receive a gentle idle motion while keeping their actual palette. Tickers preserve case and internal spaces, with a ten-character input limit and no ticker-uniqueness requirement. Uploaded coin artwork and social links remain independent of the launch character. The profile has no recent-adventures panel, and appearance changes autosave. Creator console is removed from navigation and routes.

Gym pages replace the decorative pixel building with a compact leader identity, a complete mascot on a small stage, current support/contribution information, and a clean leaderboard. Gym detail combines identity, rank, contribution metrics, and the support action, followed by Overview, Trainers, and Gym badge buttons. Available directory profiles link from the trainer roster. The support and score rules are unchanged.

The Battle arena separates Offline practice from Online setup. Its refreshed lobby uses an arena court, a labelled possible matchup, the player’s collection, and a challenge selector instead of a large generic split hero. Rival previews respond to difficulty; the party remains editable before battle. Offline players select up to three Pokémon and an Easy, Medium, Hard, or Master bot. Rival species differ from the selected team and their strength scales with the difficulty. The interface exposes matchups, move effects, PP, health, switching, and turn-by-turn feedback; status and setup moves make tactical choices matter. Practice never changes ownership or balances. Online friendly and wager controls explain the intended match while clearly showing that the server is not connected; matchmaking is disabled.

Responsive rules target desktop, laptop, tablet, and narrow mobile widths. The mobile navigation becomes a drawer. Controls include keyboard focus styles, named buttons, modal focus management, Escape handling, a skip link, and result announcements.

This remains an offline-capable frontend preview. Seeded prices, holdings, reward tables, reserves, community events, and other trainers are examples. Trades and payouts do not move funds. The 151-species catalogue is complete, but battle mechanics and learnsets remain a curated subset pending the backend. Build/model/DOM verification does not replace desktop and mobile visual review, particularly for sprite sizing, responsive navigation, and animated transitions.
