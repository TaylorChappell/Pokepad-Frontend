# Design and preview notes · interface v8

The updated theme combines warm cream, coral, soft green, sky blue, and yellow with pixel game artwork, framed game-like panels, and a persistent navigation rail. Discover uses illustrated Pokémon artwork to introduce the launchpad while keeping coin discovery and the main token prominent. Gym rankings live on the Gyms page. Search, notifications, and account access remain in the header. The primary rail order is Discover, Create Coin, Portfolio, Rewards, and Analytics; trainer and league destinations follow.

The approved Poképad identity pairs a coral-and-cream Poké Ball on a navy launch pad with rounded navy lettering and a coral accent. The header and loader render one transparent combined lockup, preserving the intended relationship between symbol and lettering. The standalone emblem is used for the favicon and main-token image. The existing Pokémon font files remain bundled in the project. Pixel display fonts support labels while ordinary text stays readable. Solana values use the bundled Solana brand mark. Asset origins are recorded in public/assets/ASSET-SOURCES.md.

Discover cards separate the token's uploaded image from its Pokémon mascot. The image or neutral monogram sits with the name and ticker; the larger mascot animates on hover or keyboard focus without changing its frame geometry. Sprite-sheet playback pauses and resumes in place; full-sprite GIF exceptions crossfade between their mounted layers. A compact line graph sits below the mascot. Cards emphasize market cap, with volume, holders, reward mode, social links, age, and an independent watchlist control. Sorting uses a restrained selected treatment. Watchlists are local, wallet-scoped preferences and can filter the discovery list. The sample USD conversion is fixed fixture data, never a live quote. Card trends consume optional ordered `Coin.priceHistory` samples. Known demo coins can display deterministic illustrative trends, labelled in the graphic’s accessible text; missing live history stays empty rather than becoming a fabricated chart.

Pixel Pokémon are the default. All 151 Gen 1 species have local animated sprite sheets derived from their Black/White sprites; these are not original Red/Blue animation frames. Drawn artwork remains available through Settings. Idle animation respects reduced-motion preferences and pauses for offscreen sprites where supported.

The sidebar partner always uses pixel art. It responds to petting, selected reward/evolution/gym events, and periodically offers short tips. Trainer avatars crop classic FireRed/LeafGreen character sprites to shoulder portraits with selectable accessory-colour variants. The character and colour are randomized on the first preview load and persist afterward. Display names fall back to a short wallet address. The header portrait now shows only the trainer. Brock and Blue use pixel-aligned SVG clothing overlays over unchanged source PNGs, with a base colour and darker garment shading so their choices visibly differ without recolouring skin or hair. Pokémon fit their stages using opaque-art bounds rather than the transparent source canvas, and My PC centers the complete silhouette. Normal and shiny palettes remain visible collection variants; Electric badges use a clear yellow.

The startup screen uses the moving Poké Ball alone, without orbiting dots. The wider red Pokédex-inspired header search control uses a generated magnifying-glass icon with a distinct handle and opens a smooth dialog with sequential orange/yellow/green lights, keyboard navigation, Escape dismissal, and results for both coins and trainers. Trainer-wallet results open public collection profiles. The demo only knows its fixture trainers and the current account; unknown wallets show a missing-profile state. Connected searches use the public trainer-directory adapter and show errors instead of falling back to fixtures.

Eggs use local pixel art with distinct tier colours, a source-coin badge, a timer, and a walking equivalent. Pack and egg reveals lead into animated Pokémon results with looping stars. Evolution has a separate transformation sequence. Reduced-motion settings shorten or remove motion. Source text is kept to “From” and the coin identity.

The How-to guide replaces the short three-page manual with nine searchable categories: getting started; coins and launching; eggs, packs, and walking; collection; training; value and marketplace; gyms; battles; and account preferences. Each category uses a relevant static Pokémon illustration, readable explanations, and direct links to the screens it describes. The full guide is also embedded in account help. It distinguishes intended live mechanics from actions supported by this frontend. Generic all-caps promotional subtitles and decorative sparkle glyphs are absent from shared page headings.

Analytics combines compact snapshot metrics, a coin-volume ranking, a reward-mode breakdown, and a filterable event list. The account activity tab reflects recorded actions. A separate Community preview tab labels its example pack reveals, hatches, purchases, evolutions, and launches; these examples do not affect the totals. Snapshot coverage and free-pool accounting are explained next to the figures.

Inventory means currently owned Pokémon; the Pokédex remembers every discovered species. My PC defaults to Ready first: actionable evolution and move-learning opportunities sort first, with newest acquisition as the tie-breaker. A coloured edge and readiness ribbon make those cards stand out. Explicit Newest, Highest value and Highest level sorts remain available. Listed or battle-locked Pokémon with a pending move show “Move waiting” without a readiness highlight or priority. Pokémon detail keeps a full sprite, identity, HP, value, and primary actions beside a Stats/Moves/Evolution/Details button menu. Information appears one section at a time, reducing vertical travel. The styled move-ready prompt identifies the offered move and its type and opens the existing learning dialog. It retains an explicit back button, Rare Candy progress, funded evolution readiness, and one-time move-learning prompts. A player can learn into an empty slot, replace one of four moves, or permanently decline. Future evolutions appear as grey silhouettes and question marks, with their names hidden from the pre-reveal interface. Eevee's three silhouettes retain equal one-in-three odds, and its outcome is random rather than a selectable branch. Funding explanations have been removed from the summary and confirmation, while insufficient-funding and other unavailability reasons still block the action. See the backend contract for funding and progression rules; these are not the original games' complete mechanics.

Create coin is a dedicated navigation destination with four steps: Details, Partner, Rewards, and Review. Optional social links expand directly beneath the description in Details. Collapsing the disclosure preserves values, invalid links keep the fields visible, and editing links from Review returns to the expanded inputs. Partner always offers the current trainer as well as eligible owned Pokémon. Back and Next occupy a persistent footer outside the step’s scroll region; compact layouts and contained reward illustrations keep controls reachable without page scrolling on typical desktop/laptop sizes. Smaller viewports retain internal scrolling rather than clipping fields. Its previews animate; static shiny assets receive a gentle idle motion while keeping their actual palette. Tickers preserve case and internal spaces, with a ten-character input limit and no ticker-uniqueness requirement. Uploaded coin artwork and social links remain independent of the launch character. The profile has no recent-adventures panel, and appearance changes autosave. Creator console is removed from navigation and routes.

Gym pages replace the decorative pixel building with a compact leader identity, a complete mascot on a small stage, current support/contribution information, and a clean leaderboard. Gym detail combines identity, rank, contribution metrics, and the support action, followed by Overview, Trainers, and Gym badge buttons. Available directory profiles link from the trainer roster. The support and score rules are unchanged.

The Battle arena separates Offline practice from Online setup. The lobby, paginated team selection and match use a viewport-constrained shell. Offline players select up to three Pokémon and an Easy, Medium, Hard or Master bot. Rival species differ from the selected team and their strength scales with difficulty. Practice never changes ownership or balances. Online friendly and wager controls show that the server is not connected; matchmaking remains disabled.

The match view follows the supplied Gen III references: rival front sprite at the upper right, player back sprite at the lower left, opposing HP panels, and a fixed bottom command area. Fight and Pokémon are the only root battle commands. The move menu shows up to four moves with type, PP and tactical details; the party menu replaces the same command area. Health, dialogue and effects play through the resolved event sequence rather than jumping directly to the end of the turn. The 48 currently playable moves each have a dedicated six-frame pixel sprite strip, with per-move timing, emission point, trajectory and impact choreography. The renderer uses nearest-neighbour raster frames rather than smooth vector attack shapes. Beams follow the measured source-to-target path; physical impacts, ground attacks, self effects, charging and draining use their corresponding anchors. Sprite placement and the platforms share one measured scene. Source-alpha bounds and anatomy profiles for all 151 species determine fitting and emission points, with distinct front/back and shiny bounds. Idle and attack body motion is layered separately from atlas cropping so it cannot change the sampled cell. Reduced motion simplifies this presentation. This is a Gen III-inspired interface over the existing simplified practice engine, not a pixel-perfect cartridge port or a complete 165-move Gen 1 ruleset.

Battle artwork uses the actual FireRed/LeafGreen front and back sprites for all 151 species and both normal/shiny palettes, stored in four local SVG atlases. A front sprite is never mirrored to imitate a back view. The soundtrack is the HeartGold/SoulSilver Kanto trainer battle recording, with its intro and loop segment. Playback starts from a battle-button gesture, has a persistent mute preference, pauses while hidden, and stops when leaving the match. Synthesized effects vary by move family and move; they are not claimed to be original cartridge sound samples.

Responsive rules target desktop, laptop, tablet, and narrow mobile widths. The mobile navigation becomes a drawer. Controls include keyboard focus styles, named buttons, modal focus management, Escape handling, a skip link, and result announcements.

This remains an offline-capable frontend preview. Seeded prices, holdings, reward tables, reserves, community events, and other trainers are examples. Trades and payouts do not move funds. The 151-species catalogue is complete, but battle mechanics and learnsets remain a curated subset pending the backend. Build/model/DOM verification does not replace desktop and mobile visual review, particularly for sprite sizing, responsive navigation, and animated transitions.

Browser QA was attempted for interface v8 but the available browser could not open the preview under its URL policy. Source, model and DOM checks do not establish exact visual matching or audible playback; desktop/mobile review in a normal browser remains necessary.
